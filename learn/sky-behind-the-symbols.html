Orientation

Part 0 · The one idea everything rests on

There are two zodiacs — Jyotiṣa uses the second

Tropical (sāyana)

0° Aries is pinned to the spring equinox — where the Sun crosses the celestial equator going north (~21 March). Tied to the seasons. Western astrology uses this.

Sidereal (nirayana)

0° Aries is pinned to an actual point among the stars (near Revatī / ζ Piscium). Tied to the fixed stars. Jyotiṣa uses this.

The two once coincided (~3rd–5th c. CE) and have drifted apart ever since at ~50″ a year. That gap — today ≈24° — is the Ayanāṁśa.
Teaching note

The single distinction the whole deck rests on. Outer gold ring = sidereal, locked to a star. Inner teal ring = tropical, locked to the equinox Sun. The shaded wedge between their 0° Aries marks is the ayanāṁśa, ~24 degrees today.

Part 1

Ayanāṁśa

The precession offset — the slow wobble of the Earth's axis, and the number that turns a chart Vedic.

Part 1 · 1.1 — The astronomy

The Earth spins like a top — and wobbles like one

The Earth's axis is tilted 23.4°, and like a slowing top that tilted axis wobbles in a slow circle — the precession of the equinoxes.

One turn ≈ 25,772 years

The axis traces a full cone once in a “Great Year.”

≈ 50.29″ per year → 1° / 71.6 yrs

The equinox point slides backward through the stars — one whole sign every ~2,150 yrs.

The cause is gravity: the Sun and Moon tug on the Earth's equatorial bulge — the same physics as a gyroscope leaning against gravity. It is why the March-equinox Sun, in Aries to the Greeks, now rises in Pisces.

Teaching note

The spinning-top Earth: tilt 23.4 degrees, the axis sweeping a dashed cone once every ~25,772 years, the equinox point sliding backward at ~50 arcsec/year. This backward slide is the entire origin of the ayanāṁśa.

Part 1 · 1.2 — The definition

What the Ayanāṁśa is

ayana (movement) + aṁśa (part). The Ayanāṁśa is simply the angular gap, right now, between tropical 0° Aries and sidereal 0° Aries.

Sidereal longitude
= Tropical longitude − Ayanāṁśa

This is exactly why one person is “Aries” in a Western app and “Pisces” in a Vedic one — same sky, different reference point.

Worked example

A planet at 10° tropical Aries
Ayanāṁśa = 24°
10° − 24° = −14°
→ 16° sidereal Pisces

Same body, same sky — a different sign, because the zero-point differs.

Teaching note

The single most useful formula in the deck. Sidereal = tropical minus ayanāṁśa. Walk the worked example slowly; the sign change from Aries to Pisces is the aha moment for anyone coming from Western astrology.

Teaching point: near a sign boundary the choice flips the sign, house or nakṣatra — so naming the ayanāṁśa is part of naming a chart. Indian Jyotiṣa (and the Madheswaran tradition) uses Lahiri.
Teaching note

Values are approximate for 2025 and rounded to the minute. Lahiri (Chitrapakṣa) is the Government of India standard, defined by keeping Chitrā (Spica) at 180°; the others are a few minutes to about 1½° away from it.

Part 1 · 1.4 — Impact

Where the choice matters: low vs high sensitivity

Low sensitivity

Planets sitting comfortably in the middle of a sign.

No reasonable ayanāṁśa choice ever moves them out of it — the reading is stable.

High sensitivity

Anything within ~1–2° of a sign boundary.

A planet, the Lagna, or a nakṣatra edge — a different ayanāṁśa flips the sign, the house, the nakṣatra, and so the whole reading.

This is why birth-time accuracy and ayanāṁśa choice matter most for the Lagna and the Moon — the two fastest-moving reference points in the chart.
Teaching note

The practical takeaway of Part 1: the ayanāṁśa only bites near boundaries. That is exactly where the Lagna and Moon live and move fastest, so that is where birth-time precision earns its keep.

Part 2

Astaṅga

Combustion — “the burning.” When a planet nears the Sun in the sky, its own light is drowned in glare.

Part 2 · 2.1 — The astronomy

Astaṅga: a planet lost in the Sun's glare

astam = setting. When a planet's longitude comes very close to the Sun's, it rises and sets almost with the Sun and is drowned in its glare — invisible, though physically still there.

Apparent, not physical

It is nearness to the Sun in the sky, not in space.

Cazimi — the heart of the Sun

Within ~16′ of the Sun's centre — effectively on its disk.

A planet you cannot see is a planet whose significations are “burnt” — like a candle beside a floodlight.
Teaching note

The faded planet inside the Sun's halo is combust; the bright one outside is free. Stress that this is apparent proximity in the sky — the exact same optical idea we will reuse for a node covering a planet in Part 5.

Part 2 · 2.3 — Impact

Not a switch but a gradient — the closer, the deeper the burn

Sun 0° orb edge Deep burn 0–3° Moderate — to about half the orb Nearly free (e.g. 17° for Mars)

The Sun's agenda is served

A combust planet still delivers results tied to the Sun's houses — authority, father, government, soul — even as its own themes dim.

The Moon is a special case

Weighted mainly by pakṣa-bala (brightness): a full Moon far from the Sun is strong, a new Moon near it weak. Combustion and phase are one fact — distance from the Sun.

Teaching note

The combustion gradient for one planet. Two nuances the classics add: the Sun's significations still come through a burnt planet, and the Moon is judged by phase (pakṣa-bala), which is the same underlying fact — distance from the Sun.

Part 3

Grahaṇa

Eclipses — why they are rare, and why they happen only at the two crossing points we call the nodes.

Part 3 · 3.1 — The astronomy

Why eclipses are rare — one small tilt

An eclipse needs Sun–Earth–Moon in a line. So why not one every new & full Moon? Because the Moon's orbit is tilted ~5.14° to the ecliptic.

At most new/full Moons the Moon passes above or below the line — no shadow lands. An eclipse can only happen when it lands near a crossing point of the two orbits.

Those two crossing points are exactly Rāhu and Ketu — so an eclipse is literally “a node swallowing the luminary.”
Teaching note

The tilted orbit. The silver ring (Moon's path) is tilted ~5 degrees to the gold ecliptic, crossing it at two points — the nodes, Rāhu and Ketu. Off-node, the Moon misses the Sun–Earth line and no eclipse occurs. This slide is the hinge of the whole deck.

Teaching note

Three numbers for the rhythm of eclipses. The Saros in particular — 18 years and 11 days — is how ancient astronomers predicted eclipses without knowing the physics, purely from the repeating geometry.

Part 4

Rāhu & Ketu

The shadow planets — chāyā grahas. Not objects at all, but the two points where the Moon's orbit crosses the ecliptic.

Part 4 · 4.1 — The concept students miss

The nodes are not objects — they are intersections

There is nothing there to see. Rāhu and Ketu are the two points where the Moon's tilted orbit crosses the ecliptic.

Rāhu ☊ — the ascending node

where the Moon crosses going north (up).

Ketu ☋ — the descending node

where it crosses going south (down).

Chāyā grahas — “shadow planets.” No disk, no light, no mass: places, not things. Which is exactly why their influence feels unseen, karmic and hard to grasp.

Teaching note

Zoom on the two crossings. Rāhu = ascending node (Moon going north, up-arrow); Ketu = descending (south, down-arrow). The dashed line shows they are always exactly 180 degrees apart. They are mathematical points, not bodies — the key insight of Part 4.

Part 4 · 4.2 — How they “form”

The myth is a mnemonic for the geometry

The classical image

At the churning of the ocean of milk, the demon Svarbhānu stole a sip of the nectar of immortality. The Sun and Moon exposed him; Viṣṇu severed him with the Sudarśana — but, now immortal, he lived on as two halves: the head Rāhu and the body Ketu. Ever after he chases and swallows the luminaries — an eclipse.

Read astronomically

The “immortal head and tailless body, forever 180° apart, that seize the luminaries” is exactly the pair of lunar nodes that cause eclipses.

The story is a precise memory-aid for the geometry of Part 3.

Teaching note

The samudra-manthana myth encodes real astronomy: two halves, 180 degrees apart, immortal, seizing the Sun and Moon — a perfect description of the two lunar nodes. Myth as mnemonic, not as competitor to science.

Part 4 · 4.3 — Their motion

Always backward — the 18.6-year drum-beat

The Moon's whole tilted orbit slowly rotates backward (nodal regression, from the Sun's pull):

Retrograde ≈ 19.35° / year

The nodes move backward through the zodiac.

One full circuit ≈ 18.6 years

The “nodal period” — Rāhu returns to its birth sign every ~18–19 yrs.

This is why nodes show as retrograde in a chart, and why their daśās (Rāhu 18 yrs, Ketu 7) and transits track this beat.

Mean vs true node: mean = smooth average (classical software, Madheswaran); true = slight wobble around it. Usually within ~1.5° — matters only near a boundary. Same “name your setting” caution as the ayanāṁśa.
Teaching note

Backward drift. The Rāhu–Ketu axis is a diameter of the zodiac that regresses ~19.35 deg/year, one full circle in 18.6 years. This drum-beat sets the nodal daśās and the eclipse-repeat rhythm.

Part 4 · 4.4 — The degrees that matter

Because they are points, power is all about closeness

Highest — eclipse degrees

A luminary within a few degrees, or a planet within ≤3–5°, takes on the node's intensity strongly.

Moderate

A wider conjunction (~5–12°) or the node's dṛṣṭi (5th/7th/9th-type) — real but softer.

Low

A node alone in a sign colours its affairs by house & dispositor, without the sharp “seizing.”

Rāhu amplifies, obsesses, foreignises · Ketu subtracts, spiritualises, detaches. Head that hungers, body that renounces.
Teaching note

Orb rings around a node: red core (high, ≤3–5°), amber (moderate, 5–12°), faint outer (low). The nodes have no fixed dignity — their tone is read from the dispositor and the planets they touch. Rāhu amplifies, Ketu detaches.

Part 5

Grahaṇa-doṣa

When a node “covers” a planet — an eclipse of that planet's own light, and how it enters a prediction.

Part 5 · 5.1 — The idea

An eclipse is not only for the Sun and Moon

In a birth chart, a node sitting with any planet is said to cover (grahaṇa) it — the planet is grasta (“seized”), its significations eclipsed. Two kinds, and the honest distinction matters:

Sun/Moon + node = literal eclipse

Within the ecliptic limits, that person was born at or near an eclipse — grahaṇa-doṣa at its most real.

Any other planet = figurative eclipse

A node has no disk to hide a body — it overshadows the planet's kāraka-light, exactly as the Sun does in Astaṅga.

Teaching note

The two burnings, side by side. Astaṅga: the Sun burns a planet with light. Grahaṇa-doṣa: a node eclipses it with shadow. Be scientifically honest — only Sun/Moon + node is a literal eclipse; for other planets it is a metaphor extended from the same optical logic.

Same-sign vs same-degree: being in the same house as a node already counts; within a few degrees is where “seizing” becomes decisive. And note which side — a planet the (retrograde) node is moving toward vs away from is weighted differently, the parallel to applying vs separating combustion.
Part 5 · 5.3 — Two different eclipses

Rāhu-cover vs Ketu-cover

Rāhu covering ↑ · the hungry head that swallows

Amplifies, distorts, obsesses, foreignises: excess, ambition, worldliness, illusion (māyā), sudden rises and scandals, a craving never satisfied. The planet's affairs grow large, restless and smoke-clouded — magnified but unclear.

Ketu covering ↓ · the headless body that lets go

Subtracts, spiritualises, detaches, makes intermittent: absence, dissatisfaction, self-doubt, sudden losses, then a turn inward. The planet's affairs flicker — now present, now gone — often “already done that in a past life.”

Head that hungers, body that renounces — hold that contrast and most node-conjunctions read themselves.
Teaching note

The single most useful predictive contrast for the nodes. Rāhu covering = amplify and cloud. Ketu covering = subtract and detach. Everything else about node conjunctions flows from this polarity.

Part 5 · 5.4 — The named doṣas & yogas

When a node covers a specific graha

☉ Node + Sun — Grahaṇa / Pitṛ-doṣa

soul, ego, authority, father, eyesight; status that rises then clouds

☽ Node + Moon — Chandra-grahaṇa ★

the mind — anxiety, mood-swings, illusion, sleep, the mother

♂ Node + Mars — Aṅgāraka (with Rāhu)

drive, courage, temper — accidents, litigation, surgery

☿ Node + Mercury

speech, logic, commerce — brilliant-but-erratic thinking

♃ Node + Jupiter — Guru-Chāṇḍāla yoga

wisdom, ethics, the guru — corrupted guidance, or unorthodox genius

♀ Node + Venus

love, art, pleasure — unconventional/foreign ties, excess, secrecy

♄ Node + Saturn

duty, career, masses — chronic delays, karmic burdens, fated shifts

Not purely negative

A tight node also concentrates worldly power and originality. Judge by the planet's strength, dispositor, aspects and daśā — the whole-chart synthesis rule.

Teaching note

The named node-conjunctions. Guru-Chāṇḍāla (node + Jupiter) and Aṅgāraka (Rāhu + Mars) are the most cited. Chandra-grahaṇa (node + Moon) is the most emphasised for the mind. Always end on the caution: a strong covered planet becomes intensity and reach, not just affliction.

Part 5 · 5.5 — In practice

How grahaṇa-doṣa enters a prediction

NatalThe covered planet delivers results in a node-flavoured, unstable form for life — and the houses it owns and sits in carry the eclipse-tone (Sun+Rāhu in the 10th → a dazzling but turbulent career).
Timing (daśā)The signature switches on during the daśā/bhukti of the covered planet, the node, or the node's dispositor — a quiet planet can turn decisive the moment its node-period runs.
TransitA transiting node crossing the natal planet — or an actual eclipse on that degree — re-triggers the theme: the living echo of the birth eclipse.
RemedyStrengthen the covered planet (mantra, dāna, gemstone where advised) and propitiate the node — the same family as classical eclipse observances.
Teaching note

The four doors grahaṇa-doṣa comes through: natal (permanent flavour), daśā (when it fires), transit (re-triggers, especially a real eclipse on the degree), remedy (strengthen the planet, propitiate the node).

Synthesis

One chain of cause — all from tilted circles

  1. The axis wobbles → the equinox slides → the two zodiacs diverge → the Ayanāṁśa.
  2. A planet's apparent nearness to the Sun → lost in glare → Astaṅga.
  3. The Moon's orbit is tilted 5° → it crosses the ecliptic at two points → the nodes.
  4. A new/full Moon near a node → Grahaṇa → and the nodes, as seizing shadows, are Rāhu & Ketu.
  5. Extend the seizing to a chart: a node with any planet covers it → grahaṇa-doṣa.
The two eclipses of a planet's light — the Sun's glare (Astaṅga) and the node's shadow (grahaṇa-doṣa) — are one insight applied twice.
Teaching note

The closing synthesis. Read the five steps as one sentence. The final line is the deck's thesis: Jyotiṣa's shadow planets, its burning, its seizing and its zero-point are all faithful descriptions of real sky-motions.
