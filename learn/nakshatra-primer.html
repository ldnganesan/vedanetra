Orientation

What is a nakṣatra?

A nakṣatra is a lunar mansion — one of 27 equal stations of the zodiac, each marked by a star or star-group. The Moon passes through roughly one a day; the sidereal month is about 27.3 days, which is why the circle is divided into 27.

Older than the rāśis

The Vedas know the nakṣatras long before the twelve signs: the Taittirīya Saṁhitā and Brāhmaṇa list them with their deities, and the Atharvaveda (19.7) hymns 28, counting Abhijit. The rāśis came later; Jyotiṣa now reads both together.

The Moon's own map

The janma-nakṣatra — the mansion holding the Moon at birth — sets the starting Vimśottarī daśā (BPHS), names the child in many families, and is the basis of compatibility (porutham) and the daily pañcāṅga.

Sidereal, star-fixed

Nakṣatras are fixed to the stars, so they are always reckoned in the sidereal (nirayana) zodiac with an ayanāṁśa — here Lahiri / Chitrapakṣa, which keeps Chitrā's star, Spica, at 180°.

A pada is a navāṁśa

Each pada of 3°20′ is exactly one navāṁśa (D9) division. Counting padas from Aśvinī-1, the navāṁśa signs run Aries, Taurus, Gemini… and repeat every 12 padas — so Aśvinī-1, Maghā-1 and Mūla-1 all fall in Aries navāṁśa. The tooltips on the grid show each pada's navāṁśa.

Why the nakṣatras and signs mesh

12 signs × 9 padas = 108 = 27 nakṣatras × 4 padas. Every sign therefore holds exactly nine padas, and the pattern of whole and split nakṣatras repeats three times around the wheel — once for each of the three fire signs that open a cycle: Aries, Leo and Sagittarius.

The degree of a planet gives its sign, its nakṣatra, its pada and its navāṁśa all at once — four readings from one number.
The nine lords · Vimśottarī (BPHS)

Nine lords, three rounds

Each nakṣatra has a lord. Starting from Aśvinī, the nine grahas take the nakṣatras in a fixed order — Ketu, Venus, Sun, Moon, Mars, Rāhu, Jupiter, Saturn, Mercury — and the cycle runs three times. The same order, with its years, is the Vimśottarī daśā of 120 years.

The pattern

Every split nakṣatra belongs to the Sun, Mars or Jupiter — the third, fifth and seventh lords in each round. The Sun's nakṣatras split 1 + 3, Mars's 2 + 2, and Jupiter's 3 + 1.

Gaṇḍānta — where both edges meet

At three points a sign boundary and a nakṣatra boundary coincide: Āśleṣā → Maghā (Cancer → Leo), Jyeṣṭhā → Mūla (Scorpio → Sagittarius) and Revatī → Aśvinī (Pisces → Aries). These water-to-fire junctions are the classical gaṇḍānta, treated with care in birth and muhūrta.

Yoni — the animal

Each nakṣatra has an animal nature, paired male and female: horse (Aśvinī, Śatabhiṣā), elephant (Bharaṇī, Revatī), sheep (Kṛttikā, Puṣya), serpent (Rohiṇī, Mṛgaśīrṣa), dog (Ārdrā, Mūla), cat (Punarvasu, Āśleṣā), rat (Maghā, P.Phalgunī), cow (U.Phalgunī, U.Bhādrapadā), buffalo (Hasta, Svātī), tiger (Citrā, Viśākhā), deer (Anurādhā, Jyeṣṭhā), monkey (P.Āṣāḍhā, Śravaṇa), lion (Dhaniṣṭhā, P.Bhādrapadā) and mongoose (U.Āṣāḍhā alone).

Used in yoni-kūṭa (yoni porutham).

BPHS supplies the lords and the pada–navāṁśa link; the deities come from the Vedic Saṁhitās; gaṇa, yoni and the seven natures come from the muhūrta texts (Bṛhat Saṁhitā, Muhūrta Cintāmaṇi).
