THE ZODIAC & THE SIGNS

A circle of 360°, twelve signs of 30°

360° = 12 × 30°

The Zodiac (rāśi-cakra) — the boundless star-belt — is a circle of 360°, divided into 12 equal parts of 30° each. Those twelve parts are the twelve rāśis (signs).

The 12 steps of Jyotiṣa

These twelve rāśis are 'the twelve steps (paḍikkaṟkaḷ) of Jyotiṣa śāstra' — the mirror of time (kāla kaṇṇāḍi) that shows the state of a human life.

Nine grahas act within them

The nine grahas (navagrahas) move through these twelve signs, producing every effect. 'To know them all completely — our lifetime would not suffice,' the author writes.

Teaching note

The opening definition. Keep it concrete: one full circle, sliced into twelve equal 30° arcs. The twelve dots on the ring are the twelve sign-boundaries. The nine grahas travel this ring; where each sits and what it aspects is the whole of chart reading.

READING A CHART

The South-Indian rāśi-cakra

Read clockwise from the left

We reckon iḍam → valam (left → clockwise). Whichever sign holds the Ascendant is marked 'Lagnam'; the houses are then counted on from there.

READING A CHART

How the houses are counted

The rāśi is the frame; the Lagna is the start

Find the sign on the eastern horizon at birth — that is the Lagna (Ascendant). Mark it '1', then count the 2nd, 3rd … 12th houses on around the fixed wheel.

Why the author raises this

At a Jyotiṣa conference in Coimbatore, a North-Indian astrologer explained charts in the North-Indian format and the audience couldn't follow — some had to ask for help. Because that format is unfamiliar in Tamil Nadu, he introduces all the regional styles so a reader can use a chart from any tradition or software.

Worked example — Cancer Ascendant

Cancer sits as the 4th square in the fixed chart. For a Cancer-lagna native you write 'Lagnam' there, then number outward — 2nd, 3rd … — to the 12th. 'It doesn't read naturally at first,' the author says, 'but habit makes it easy.'

Teaching note

The house-numbering rule and the Cancer example straight from p.9. The teaching point: the SIGN wheel is fixed, but the HOUSE numbering starts wherever the Lagna falls and runs on around it. The conference anecdote is why he covers other regions next.

READING A CHART

The four regional chart formats

South Indian

Square; signs FIXED (Pisces top-left), planets move; read clockwise. Used in Tamil Nadu.

North Indian

Diamond; HOUSES fixed (Lagna always top-centre), signs move; read counter-clockwise.

Bengali (Vaṅgāḷam)

Diagonal (X) diagram; whichever rāśi is the Lagna is marked as such within its cell.

Oriya (Orissa)

A similar diagram; reckoned — as we do — from the left, clockwise (valamāga).

One and the same horoscope — four ways of drawing it. Only the visual convention differs; the planetary placement is identical.
Teaching note

The four styles the author walks through (pp.9–10). Same chart, different picture. Modern relevance: software and books from different traditions use different formats, so a student should recognise all four. South vs North is the big divide — signs-fixed vs houses-fixed.

CLASSIFYING THE SIGNS

Solar and lunar signs

Sūrya-rāśis (solar)

Because Leo is the Sun's own sign, the six signs Leo → Capricorn are the solar rāśis.

Leo · Virgo · Libra · Scorpio · Sagittarius · Capricorn

Candra-rāśis (lunar)

Because Cancer is the Moon's own sign, the six signs Cancer → Aquarius (clockwise) are the lunar rāśis.

Cancer · Leo* · … · Aquarius (the lunar half)

The zodiac divides into a solar half and a lunar half, meeting at the Cancer–Leo axis — the symmetry behind the day/night (divā/rātri) strength of signs.
Teaching note

The sūrya/candra division (p.11): the Sun's own sign is Leo, so the six from Leo onward are solar; the Moon's own sign is Cancer, so the six from Cancer are lunar. It underlies the day/night strength of signs. (The two halves overlap at the Cancer–Leo hinge — that's the mirror-point of the wheel.)

CLASSIFYING THE SIGNS

Movable, fixed and dual

Chara — சரம் (movable)

Aries · Cancer · Libra · Capricorn

Initiating, mobile, quick to start — the four 'cardinal' signs.

Sthira — ஸ்திரம் (fixed)

Taurus · Leo · Scorpio · Aquarius

Steady, stubborn, enduring — the four 'fixed' signs.

Ubhaya — உபயம் (dual)

Gemini · Virgo · Sagittarius · Pisces

Adaptable, two-natured, mediating — the four 'mutable' signs.

Why it matters later: the bādhaka-sthāna (affliction house) is the 11th from a movable lagna, the 9th from a fixed lagna, and the 7th from a dual lagna. Several sign-decks flag their bādhaka on exactly this rule.
Teaching note

The chara/sthira/ubhaya split (p.11) — four signs each. Beyond temperament, this is the key to the bādhaka-sthāna, which recurs in the sign-decks: 11th for movable, 9th for fixed, 7th for dual lagnas. Have students memorise the three groups of four.

CLASSIFYING THE SIGNS

Rāśi-māna — how long a sign rises

Rising times vary

Each sign takes a different length of time to rise, and that length shifts with the ayanāṁśa. The northern and southern ayanāṁśa rāśi-measures differ.

Don't use round numbers

The old textbook figures — Aries 4¼, Taurus 4¾, Gemini 5½ nāḻikai — should NOT be used directly. Reckon by the ayanāṁśa-based rāśi-māna from the ephemeris.

Read the pañcāṅga

The precise rising-times sit in the last column of each month in the Dṛk-gaṇita (Thirukkaṇita) pañcāṅga, under 'rāśi-māna iruppu' (see its p.72).

Worked example: if on Chittirai 1 the Aries rāśi-māna is 4.31 nāḻikai, that measure is shared across the month, shrinking ~10 seconds a day, until on Vaikāsi 1 the Taurus measure begins in full at 5.02.
Teaching note

The rising-time discussion (pp.11–12). The practical rule: never use the round textbook numbers — use ephemeris (dṛk-gaṇita) values, which depend on ayanāṁśa and latitude. This is why an accurate Lagna needs an accurate birth time and a good pañcāṅga/software.

THE HEART OF THE METHOD

Lagna, not rāśi

"Though we say rāśi, since we speak of the ātman — the living self — it should properly be called the Lagna. This book is the effects of the twelve Lagna-bhāvas."

Lagna (உதயலக்னம்)

The Ascendant — the sign rising in the east at birth. Body, self, life-direction. This book's organising frame.

Chandra-lagna

The Moon-sign — mind and emotions. In everyday speech 'rāśi' means this: the house the Moon sits in.

Sūrya-lagna

The Sun-sign — soul and vitality. The third reference point of the classical tri-lagna reading.

Teaching note

The book's key methodological statement (p.12) and the reason for its title. Classical Jyotiṣa reads from THREE points — Lagna (body), Chandra-lagna (mind), Sūrya-lagna (soul). Phaladīpikā and BPHS both judge from the Lagna first and confirm from the Moon. This book takes the Lagna as its frame — one Ascendant per chapter.

VOCABULARY YOU'LL NEED

Planetary dignities — a primer

Uccham — உச்சம் (exaltation)

A planet at its strongest sign — best results.

Nīcam — நீசம் (debilitation)

A planet at its weakest sign — results reduced.

Mūlatrikoṇa

A special strong arc within a planet's own sign.

Sva-kṣetra (own sign)

A planet in a sign it rules — comfortable, strong.

Yogakāraka

For a given lagna, a planet owning both a kendra and a trikoṇa — a great benefic.

Kendrādhipati-doṣa

A natural benefic owning only kendras can turn unhelpful for that lagna.

Teaching aid — these terms recur in every sign-deck; a planet is judged first by its dignity, then by the houses it owns for that lagna.
Teaching note

A primer of the strength/lordship vocabulary the sign-decks assume (standard Jyotiṣa, e.g. Bṛhat Jātaka / BPHS — not from this intro chapter, but essential scaffolding). Uccha/nīca/mūlatrikoṇa/own-sign are about a planet's dignity; yogakāraka and kendrādhipati-doṣa are about what it owns for a particular lagna.

VOCABULARY YOU'LL NEED

Key yogas — a primer

Rāja yoga

A wealth-and-power combination — typically a kendra-lord with a trikoṇa-lord.

Dhana yoga

A wealth combination — lords of the 2nd, 5th, 9th, 11th linking up.

Neechabhaṅga Rāja yoga

A debilitated planet's weakness is cancelled — turning a nīca into strength.

Pañca Mahāpuruṣa yoga

One of five 'great person' yogas — e.g. Bhadra (Mercury), Haṃsa (Jupiter) — a planet strong in a kendra.

Gaja-Kesari

Jupiter in a kendra from the Moon — fame and good fortune.

Māraka & Bādhaka

Houses/lords that can harm: māraka (2nd & 7th) touch longevity; bādhaka (11th/9th/7th by modality) obstruct.

Teaching aid — the yogas named across the series in one place; each sign-deck shows which of them a given lagna can form.

A quick reference for the yogas the decks mention (standard classical material — Phaladīpikā, Sārāvalī, BPHS — added as scaffolding, not from this intro chapter). Don't drill all of them now; point students back here when a specific yoga comes up in a sign-deck.

THE SERIES

How to use these decks

Start here, then pick a sign

Use this opener for the shared foundations, then open the deck for the Ascendant you're teaching.

One factor is only a hint

Every single placement is indicative. Confirm with strength, aspects, the vargas (esp. D9/D10) and the running daśā.

Watch the framing

The book keeps the source's male-default voice and period idiom; read the portraits as the author's framing, not prescriptions.

Added notes are boxed

In the written drafts, anything beyond the source is clearly marked '➕ Added note' with a classical reference (BPHS, Bṛhat Jātaka, Phaladīpikā, Sārāvalī …).

Teaching note

Ground rules for teaching from the series. The big one: single-factor readings are hints, never verdicts — always synthesise. Note the fidelity conventions (source framing kept; additions boxed and sourced) so students know what's the book and what's scholarly context.

WRAP-UP

Key takeaways

1

The zodiac is 360°, twelve signs of 30° — the twelve steps of Jyotiṣa.

2

Seven planets rule the signs; Sun→Leo, Moon→Cancer, the other five a pair each. Rāhu/Ketu rule none.

3

The South-Indian chart fixes the signs and moves the planets; houses count from the Lagna, clockwise.

4

Signs group as chara/sthira/ubhaya — which fixes the bādhaka (11th/9th/7th).

5

Rising-times (rāśi-māna) come from the ephemeris, not round numbers — so birth time matters.

6

This book reads from the LAGNA (the ātman), not the sun- or moon-sign — hence 'Lagna-bhāva palan'.

Teaching note

Recap the foundation. The one line to leave them with: this whole series reads from the Lagna. Everything else — signs, lords, chart formats, classifications, rising-times — is the literacy that makes a lagna-reading possible.
