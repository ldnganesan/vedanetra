Mīnam at a glance

Symbol

Element / class

Water-natured (jala) · saumya (benign) · a dharma sign · the mokṣa-sign of the zodiac

Modality

12th & last sign of the zodiac · signifies the feet

Anchor the image: the two fishes, water, Jupiter, the feet. Dual + water + Jupiter predicts a compassionate, devout, imaginative, elusive nature. The headlines: like Sagittarius, no planet is an enemy here; Venus is exalted, Mercury fallen; and this is the 12th, the mokṣa-sign closing the zodiac.

The yoga-givers

Jupiter (1st + 10th, lagna + karma), Moon (5th trine) & Mars (2nd + 9th trine) — the sign's uplifting planets.

The rāja-yoga crescendo

Moon+Mars = Chandra-Maṅgala; Moon+Jupiter = Gaja-Kesari; all together → a rāja yoga.

Bādhaka — 7th

Dual signs take the 7th as bādhaka; Mercury (debilitated) is the bādhakādhipati.

Māraka — 2nd & 7th

Mars (2nd) & Mercury (7th) — the standard; the author stresses Mercury & Venus as the malefics.

Teaching note

Internalise the lordships. Jupiter owns the lagna & the 10th; Moon (5th) & Mars (9th) own the trines = yogakāras. Their conjunctions build the rāja yoga. Bādhaka = 7th (Mercury, correct for a dual sign); māraka = 2nd & 7th standard, with the author stressing Mercury & Venus as the functional malefics.

THE NATIVE'S PORTRAIT

Classical view · two authorities

Phaladīpikā

Drinks much water; a flawless, beautiful body; unites only with his own wife; an enjoyer of water-born goods; learned; forgets no kindness done; conquers his enemies; and fortunate.

Satyāchārya

Thick lips, fish-like eyes & a big nose; a kapha-wind body; of a high, noble nature; skin diseases; does intelligent deeds; interested in dharma; gives pride to his parents; magnanimous; respected in assemblies.

Teaching note

Read as period portraits — the gentlest in the book. Phaladīpikā catches the water-loving, faithful, learned, grateful, fortunate nature; Satyāchārya the noble, dharma-minded, magnanimous, assembly-respected one. Both fit the benevolent final sign.

THE NATIVE'S PORTRAIT

Classical view · Jātaka Alaṅkāram

Born in the beautiful Mīna lagna — abundant wealth; a valorous, handsome body; a sannyāsi; two wives; great love for fragrant flowers; desires others' goods; great affection for relatives; eager to be foremost; a food-lover; a little blunt.

THE NATIVE'S PORTRAIT

From experience · the two fishes

The two fishes

The symbol — two fishes, head to tail — gives a slippery nature: they slip out of anything, hard to trap; 'cast a net and they cut it and escape.'

Work over words

They speak very little, listening quietly 'like sages,' and do only what their own mind decides — giving speech little weight and work much, planning and completing precisely, without fault.

The compassionate philanthropist

Of a compassionate mind (irakka-cintai); their magnanimity shows in public and divine works; they help — by money, labour or their word — once they know a person's real need.

Honest, and not to be fooled

Honest, taking no unfair profit; they seem naive but are not, seem timid but are not — 'that too is a false front'; they fear no one, and repay a wrong in kind.

Teaching note

The author's central image: the two fishes — elusive, work-over-words, compassionate and honest, but not the fool or coward they appear. The 'slip out of any net' image and the compassion (irakka-cintai) are the heart of this gentlest of portraits.

THE NATIVE'S PORTRAIT

From experience · service, devotion, comfort

Sought as elders

They can be town-elders and headmen; unable to bear injustice, but lacking courage to confront it directly, they file a petition instead.

Thrifty, yet giving

A little thrifty and self-interested — yet, mostly without their own money, they get their work done from savings, and gain a good name from public and divine works.

The cool, water-loving body

Of a cool, watery constitution — drinking much water, loving cool things and sweets, unable to bear great heat or cold; they take work needing little physical labour.

Devotion & the art of love

Deep in Veda, yoga, dhyāna and bhakti; experts in the art of love, of long-lasting potency, fully satisfying the wife — and, given the chance, others, though not wandering for them.

Teaching note

The other half: the sought-after elder who petitions rather than confronts, the thrifty-yet-giving philanthropist, the cool water-loving body, and the devotion-and-pleasure blend. Handle the desire material maturely as the text's framing. Being a female sign, the author's voice is male-default.

Jupiter (Guru)

குரு
Lord · 1st & 10th

The lord at home — magnanimous, but thrifty.

  • Gives magnanimity in abundance — but with a little more thrift: deliberating ten times before spending, and trusting no one, keeping every expense under their own watch.
  • 'Is money a tree, fruiting on plants? — spend by count,' they say; they love all at home, especially the children, and are strict only in money-matters.
  • Generally trustworthy — they will not cheat or deceive; deep interest in Veda-śāstra, yoga, dhyāna and bhakti, front-rank in divine works and pilgrimages.
  • Desire is high, needing the wife's nearness daily; the wife (7th = Mercury's Virgo) is skilled and capable, winning them by the bed's charm.
Teaching note

The lord in its own sign, owning the lagna & the 10th (karma) — a strong, benevolent, trustworthy, devout placement, thrifty only with money. The archetype of the honourable, dharma-minded Piscean.

Sun (Sūrya)

சூரியன்

Functional enemy · 6th lord

  • Not special. Though a friend by nature, as the 6th (dusthāna) lord it makes them hasty and angry, changing Mīna's natural calm.
  • It makes them blurt, careless and proud, changing their nature, and — by random spending — turns them into debtors.
  • Body-health is poor, attacked by disease often (a water-and-fire mix bringing chest and pitta disorders) — 'always some disease in the body.'
  • Desire is high; from excess union come body-decline, nerve-weakness, trembling of the hands and legs, and pitta-fever.
Teaching note

The Sun owns the 6th (a dusthāna) for Pisces, so — though a natural friend — it acts as a functional enemy, disturbing the sign's calm with haste, pride and ill-health. Take it as neutral-to-difficult.

Moon (Candra)

சந்திரன்

Friend · yogakāra · 5th lord

  • An excellent state: the trikoṇa-lord Moon arranges their life well; a waxing Moon gives fame, influence, status and honour.
  • Sweet speech that pleases all, and a nature that carries everyone along; great love for the wife, motherly affection, respect for relatives and friends.
  • Dharma-quality and delight in temple service, mahān-darshan and pilgrimage; in public service, honours and headmanships come to them.
  • They fulfil duties exactly, seek no profit, spend even their own money; of long-lasting potency, relishing the wife (and, given the chance, others) — but not wandering for them.
Teaching note

The Moon owns the 5th (trikoṇa) for Pisces, so Moon-in-lagna is one of the finest single placements — fame, sweetness, dharma, honours. 'The Moon does not fail to give Mīna good results.'

Mars (Kuja)

செவ்வாய்

Friend · yogakāra · 2nd & 9th

  • The lagna-benefic and yogakāra Mars, though giving a little quick temper, makes them capable and courageous.
  • It grants professional and earning strength — though it also sets them spending a little more than they save.
  • Keen in public service, divine works and pilgrimages, front-rank and seeking no profit; they speak plainly and openly.
  • Desire is a little high, but they keep such ties hidden to guard their honour.
Teaching note

Mars owns the 9th (dharma trine) & 2nd for Pisces — a yogakāra — so Mars-in-lagna is a capable, courageous, dignified placement. With the Moon and Jupiter it builds the chapter's rāja yoga.

Mercury (Budha)

புதன்

Debilitated · bādhaka · 4th & 7th

  • Cannot be called satisfying. As the debilitated 4th & 7th lord (the bādhaka), they cannot decide quickly, growing confused.
  • They postpone and miss chances, acting back-and-forth (mun-pin murana).
  • The one thing they cannot give up is women — ever needing the wife's nearness; the wife dominates the home, and they hand it to her and withdraw.
  • Aloof from relatives, and a little lax in earning and profession.
Teaching note

Mercury falls here (Pisces is its debilitation sign) and is the bādhaka (7th) with kendrādhipati-doṣa — so Mercury-in-lagna reads as indecisive, wife-ruled and lax. Watch for neechabhaṅga in a real chart before applying literally.

Venus (Śukra)

சுக்கிரன்

Exalted · 3rd & 8th

  • A peculiar, special state: as a functional malefic (3rd & 8th lord) yet exalted, it increases longevity (āyus).
  • As the lord of world-pleasures and of the 3rd (bhoga) house, it heightens desire — 'a Manmatha (Cupid) in the matter of love' — of long-lasting potency.
  • The Venus-in-Mīna native is beautiful and refined, the wife winning the husband by her charms and bedroom-arts.
  • As the 8th lord too, they crave a pompous, comfortable life, not hesitating to borrow — big spenders (especially on women), with nerve-weakness from excess and a life of ups and downs.
Teaching note

Venus is EXALTED here — its very best sign — yet owns the 8th, so the author reads it as heightened longevity and desire rather than pure fortune: a reminder that dignity AND functional lordship must both be weighed. Present the desire material as the text's framing.

Saturn (Śani)

சனி

Neutral · 11th & 12th

  • Cannot be called special. It makes them slow and lazy, postponing everything and missing chances.
  • They sit idle and build castles in the air ('life should be like this, like that').
  • They will not so much as pull out a splinter without deliberating ten times.
  • Low concern for the family — handing it to the wife and withdrawing, aloof from relatives, lax in earning, forever philosophising 'what comes, how it goes.'
Teaching note

Saturn (11th & 12th) is neutral here but brings its manda quality — slowness, idleness, castle-building and withdrawal. Not harmful so much as inert; the sign's dreamy, detached side amplified.

Rāhu (Rāhu)

ராகு
Neutral

The Veda-minded node — shrewd and hidden.

  • Not to be underrated — generally Rāhu joins Jupiter here (Mīna being Jupiter's), giving good jñāna (a camphor-intellect), grasping anything at once.
  • Interest in vedānta, tattva, yoga, dhyāna and bhakti, in divine works, and in ties with jñānis and mahāns, and in public service.
  • They seem careless in profession and earning but are actually skilled, getting things done cleverly through others (knowing whom to use and how), not lazy.
  • Desire is high, but they satisfy it hidden.
Teaching note

Rāhu is neutral here and, joined with Jupiter in its own dharma sign, turns spiritually fruitful — a shrewd, learned, effective node whose skill hides behind an appearance of unconcern. Present descriptively.

Ketu (Ketu)

கேது
Neutral

The devout, fluent, unreadable one.

  • A little lazy — expecting others to do the small tasks — yet carrying themselves like big men.
  • Ties with jñānis, mahāns, monasteries and the bhakti-path, and interest in discourse and upanyāsa.
  • A good memory and fluent speech that quotes, draws analogies and draws people in.
  • They hide their inner matters — profession, earning, family, desire — from everyone, remaining a puzzle none can read.
Teaching note

Ketu is neutral here — the devout, fluent, crowd-drawing, deeply-private type, fitting the spiritual final sign. Rounds out a node-pair that behaves benignly for Pisces.

PART 5

Conjunctions
in Mīnam

The Sun with each planet — and the Moon-family rāja-yoga crescendo

Moon + Mars + Jupiter → Chandra-Maṅgala + Gaja-Kesari → a rāja yoga

Teaching note

The author frames the two-planet combos around the Sun; then the Moon-family combines that climb to a rāja yoga. Preview the crescendo: Moon+Mars+Jupiter forms Chandra-Maṅgala and Gaja-Kesari together — the book's final, most auspicious combine.

CONJUNCTIONS

Sun + Mars · Sun + Mercury

Sun + Mars

Both friends joined give extraordinary drive and openness — fearless, speaking plainly without hiding, of good courage, and of professional and earning strength. But quick anger and stubbornness obstruct their advancement, and — dismissing others' advice — they act on their own; high pitta, and, from unsatisfying union, nerve-weakness.

Sun + Mercury

Gives skill and shrewdness — wishing for a comfortable life and getting it even by borrowing; the wife is a big spender who dominates the home and wins them by her charms; the combine heightens desire, so they strive for the 'next' woman too. Generally it gives the comforts of life.

Teaching note

Sun+Mars = drive and openness, held back by anger. Sun+Mercury = skill and comforts, with a wife-run home and heightened desire. Handle the desire material as the text's framing.

CONJUNCTIONS

Sun + Jupiter · Sun + Venus

Sun + Jupiter

Acts in a good way — creating honour, status and a keenness for public service; guarding their honour and spending to protect it; keen on divine works, jñāni-darshan and pilgrimages; front-rank in dharma deeds — giving to the public, digging wells and ponds, running monasteries — seeking no profit and speaking plainly, though keeping desire hidden.

Sun + Venus

Not special — a great longing to enjoy every pleasure, and, lacking the means, spending lavishly even by borrowing for others' praise; at night the wife seems a goddess, but the moment another woman is available they pull her to the bed too; careless of earnings, drawn to beautiful women.

Teaching note

Sun+Jupiter is the fine one — the honourable, dharma-minded public benefactor. Sun+Venus is the pleasure-longing, borrowing, wandering one. A clean contrast. Present the desire material as the text's framing.

CONJUNCTIONS

Sun + Saturn · Sun + Rāhu/Ketu

Sun + Saturn

Does not shine — of an unsteady mind, doing nothing with care or speed, hasty yet at times very lazy, so chances slip. Low concern for profession — but women always on the mind ('the bed forever in their thoughts'); many diseases attack (nerve-weakness, pitta, joint-vāta, sex-diseases), and the father-relation is poor.

Sun + Rāhu (or Ketu)

Not special — creating very bad acts and reducing Mīna's glory: ties with the low, crooked earning, smuggling, cheating — yet posing as devotees (a false front). They claim they can do anything, cultivate a fearful reputation, and use mantrik/tāntrik work for their own rise and to trouble others.

Teaching note

Sun+Saturn = the unsteady, ill-health, poor-father one. Sun+nodes corrupt the Sun into crooked acts behind a devotee's mask. The cautionary end of the Sun-conjunctions. Present descriptively.

CONJUNCTIONS

The Moon-family combines

Moon + Mars

Very special — a waxing Moon with Mars (both yogakāras) forms Chandra-Maṅgala (śaśi-maṅgala) yoga, raising them very high: honour, status, influence, good intellect and jñāna, divine works and jñāni-ties, pilgrimages and dharma deeds.

Moon + Jupiter · Moon + Mercury

Moon + Jupiter forms Gaja-Kesari yoga — high positions, public service and honour. Moon + Mercury cannot be faulted — good skill and shrewdness, and a good, beautiful, capable wife.

Teaching note

The Moon-family climb: Moon+Mars = Chandra-Maṅgala (very high); Moon+Jupiter = Gaja-Kesari; Moon+Mercury = skill and a fine wife. These are the building blocks of the rāja-yoga crescendo on the next slide.

CONJUNCTIONS

The multi-planet combines — the crescendo

Moon + Mars + Jupiter — the crescendo: a rāja yoga, with Gaja-Kesari, Guru-Maṅgala and Chandra-Maṅgala together, making them very big men. Add Mercury (a kendra-lord with two trine-lords) → they surely rule an administration.

The rāja-yoga combines

Moon+Mars+Jupiter → history-making leaders; Mars+Jupiter → a wondrous combine (rise, public service, high positions); Sun+Moon+Mars and Sun+Mars+Moon+Jupiter are good.

The mixed combines

Mars+Mercury (firmless, self-willed); Mars+Venus (Venus spoils Mars's distinction, dragging to women & pomp); Moon+Jupiter also runs to big spending on pomp and women.

The difficult combines

Mars+Saturn (a conflict-mind, sorrowful, castle-building); Mars+Rāhu/Ketu (crooked & low, posing as devotees); Moon+Saturn & Moon+nodes (careless, crooked).

The author's close

'So far we have seen some of the combinations for the twelve lagnas — this is only a portion; there is much more. We shall meet again in the next book.'

Teaching note

The book's climax: Moon+Mars+Jupiter = a rāja yoga (Gaja-Kesari + Chandra-Maṅgala + Guru-Maṅgala), and with Mercury added (kendra-lord + two trine-lords) they rule an administration — history-making leaders. The finale deliberately ends on the zodiac's most auspicious combine, then the author's closing words. Note the difficult combines too, for balance.
